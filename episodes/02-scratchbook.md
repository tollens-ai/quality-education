## A songwriter reviewer's rewrites of v4 (2026-09-27, verbatim)

I'd fix two things before the details: the chorus and the length.

- **Chorus:** the rhythm grid (4+3+6 anapests, Qing's shape) is exactly right for MiniMax. But lines 2–4 are there for the rhyme rather than the meaning.
- **Length:** I count about 580 sung syllables. At the house budget of 3–4 a second that's 2:45–3:15 of singing before the spoken parts, fills and the 8-second instrumental. It won't land at 2:40.

The hook line is the best thing on the sheet. Its nod to "I Can't Make You Love Me" makes it cheesy in the fun way, and its "do" rhymes with verse 1's "you/clue". The genre furniture also works: the spoken dedication, "baby, baby", the text from an ex, the stools and the key change.

### Ranked problems

**1. Chorus lines 2–4 are there for the rhyme.**
- "How do I see, guarantee" doesn't parse.
- "A test to believe" isn't a real phrase, and it says "test" twice.
- "Draw the line" adds no meaning.
- "Or the bee's knees" is 7 syllables and ends on two stressed beats, where the other lines end on one. That breaks the grid in the last line before the hook.
- Line 3 should carry the whole oracle problem: the tests are green, so will *you* agree?

Fix: the rewrite below. It keeps Qing's line 1 as she wrote it.

**2. The chorus is a wall of words with nowhere to breathe.** It's four 13-syllable questions back to back, then a 13-syllable tag. Boy-band choruses breathe and answer themselves. Short backing-vocal answers are also what worked for MiniMax in episode 1. Fix: add *(ooh, baby)* after lines 1–3 and *(love it)* inside the tag. Those also mark where each phrase ends.

**3. The song is too long for 2:40.** Fix: cut the verse 2 tag, or halve the break (keep lines 1 and 3). Also drop the pre-chorus before chorus 2 and go straight in from verse 2. Losing both saves about 20 s. Better still, test-generate before cutting.

**4. Verse 2 changes rhythm halfway through.** Lines 1–2 are dactylic, with stresses on syllables 1, 4, 7, 10 and 13. Lines 3–4 land their stresses somewhere else, and the two don't match each other.
- "All I ever get's a no" is clumsy.
- "Oh, but you've got three!" is 5 syllables against 6. Worse, it follows *two* examples, so listeners count mockup and spreadsheet and hunt for the third, which only arrives in the bridge.
- "Every sum that it made" is filler, and a spreadsheet doesn't "make" sums.

Fix: the rewrite below.

**5. The two pre-chorus lines pause in different places.** Line 1 pauses after syllable 6 ("wrong,"). Line 2 pauses after syllable 7 ("see it,"). Line 2 then has "but you" where line 1 has the stressed "NEV-". Line 3 rhymes with nothing, and "brief/love it" in the rhyme notes isn't a rhyme. Fix:

> you TELL me when it's BRO-ken, NEV-er HOW to TELL it's RIGHT; [14: stresses on 2,4,6,8,10,12,14; break after 7]
> you KNOW it when you SEE it, KEEP the AN-swer OUT of SIGHT, [14: same stresses, same break]

These now match syllable for syllable. "Broken" calls back to "still broken", and "answer" sets up verse 2's "answer key". Line 3 changing in the final pre-chorus still breaks the "repeat word for word" rule. It keeps the same stresses, so it's worth the risk, but listen for it in the takes.

**6. Verse 1 line 3 is off the grid.** Lines 1, 2 and 4 stress the odd syllables. "Then you SENT me still BRO-ken, like a TEXT from an EX" stresses 3, 6, 10 and 13. Fix:

> and you TEXT-ed back "still BRO-ken", COLD, just LIKE an EX, [13: stresses on 3,7,9,11,13]

This adds a two-syllable rhyme, TEXT-ed / per-PLEXED, and the "text from an ex" joke survives as "cold, just like an ex".

**7. The bridge mostly scans, but line 2 is off and "gym bests" is a weak spot.**
- Lines 1, 3 and 4 stress syllables 3, 7 and 10. "Could your DAD use your SHOP" stresses 3, 6 and 10.
- "Gym bests dis-" sung fast comes out as "Jim Bess is".
- "Bests" on its own isn't what lifters say.

Fix:

> could your DAD buy from your SHOP, and not STRESS? [10: stresses on 3,7,10]
> if your GYM log lost your BESTS, would you SWEAT? [10: stresses on 3,7,10, the same shape as the group-chat line]

Also mark line 1's stress naturally: "if it's GOOD, SOME-one can TELL".

**8. The outro has a double meaning and mixes up two tests.**
- "PLAYED you" is slang for "tricked you". A heartbroken boy band singing "played you" will be heard that way.
- "Your bests never leave" blends the sweat test (bests survive no signal and app updates) with the group-chat test (weight never leaves the phone). That's a factual blur on screen.

Fix: the rewrite below.

**9. The song never says the dad test turns into you.** The bridge's dad is on *a shop*. The outro says the band played *you*. The step between them, that an app just for me has me as its dad test, exists only in the brief shot. Fix: in the break, change line 2's echo from *(all day long!)* to *(or play you!)*. Nothing else moves.

Break line 4 also scans differently from lines 1–3 (stresses on 2, 4, 6 against 2, 5, 9), and "yours to claim" is a filler phrase. Lower priority: half time lets it bend. Other small mishearing risks, lower priority: "app of your" can blur into "apple your".

**10. Hook stress.** It's marked "if I CAN'T tell". Sung naturally, and for the meaning, it's "if I can't TELL when you DO". Mark it that way so the generator's copy and the captions put the stress on the idea.
