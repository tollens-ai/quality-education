# How Tim Blais makes A Capella Science, and what transfers

Researched 2026-09-25 after Qing set the bar as "Tim blais style craft". Quotes marked **checked**
match YouTube's auto-generated captions word for word (check the audio before anything goes on
screen); **extract** means seen only through a summarising tool.

1. **Picture and lyric carry different parts of the story.** "what I used to do was I would I would
   sing and then I would just represent like exactly what I was singing on the screen but that's a
   real waste of space … you've got these two bandwidths coming at you at the same time so I'm what
   I'm trying to do now is more tell part of the story with the with visuals and part of the story
   with the lyrics" (checked; [S1](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Q_wdtcQ1vFw) ~16:15). For each
   line, write what the picture adds that the words don't. A shot that just illustrates the sung
   noun is a defect.
2. **Map the lesson onto the song's emotional arc; the biggest idea goes on the climax.** "take
   whatever emotional structure that your your music is fulfilling and match the like the lyrical
   content … so people are feeling the story through the words" (checked;
   [S2](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JSLeelKrThs) ~5:25). One message that "sticks the landing"
   wins.
3. **The hooks get the best work:** "what are the hooks … if you've heard the song twice here's
   what you definitely remember … those are the ones you really want to … nail to the wall"
   (checked; S2 ~6:00). Give the hook lines the most precise, repeatable picture.
4. **The visual form acts out the concept.** *Entropic Time* was shot backwards to explain time's
   arrow; "much more in your face and demonstrating as opposed to just singing about it" (checked;
   [S3](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kOdvny64sTw) ~6:05). Find a mechanic that behaves like the
   idea, not a labelled diagram.
5. **Layers for different viewers.** "I like to hide things in there for that one person who's
   like struggling … in their fifth year of a PhD … no one else gets the joke but they do"
   (checked; S1 ~8:25). A one-second surface read for newcomers; correct detail for experts.
6. **Sync visual events to musical accents** (checked; S2 ~1:02:00).
7. **Don't let performance crowd out explanation.** His own critique of *Defining Gravity*: "it
   inhibits doing a lot of the explanation via via visuals" (checked;
   [S5](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iO4k1L7mx-s) ~36:40). The singer frames and reacts; it
   steps aside when the teaching image is up.
8. **Know far more than fits:** "I have to know about 20 times more about a subject than is
   actually going to go into the song" (partly checked; S1 ~15:00). He checks with experts,
   names sources and publishes corrections.
9. **Plan every moment against song time first:** for *Entropic Time* he "spent about a week um
   mapping out the parts of this video and putting it in a huge spreadsheet" (checked;
   [S4](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=54nAOaEe_Fw) ~1:05).
10. **Borrow the source video's look** so the parody reads instantly, and spend the originality on
    the content (checked; S2 ~7:00). Less relevant to an original song.

Time: most videos take "a few hundred hours" (extract; [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tim_Blais)).
