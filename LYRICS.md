# Lyric craft

The entry point for writing and reviewing lyrics for *Software Quality Theory 101*.
Qing is the domain expert and the songwriting ear. Her dated corrections are preserved in
the focused references below and in each episode’s expert notes.

Use the [songwriting skill](.claude/skills/songwriting/SKILL.md) for the current workflow:
lyrics and phrasing grids, generated takes, selection by ear, then timing for the video.
The unsuccessful melody/harmony workflow is [archived](archive/code-composition/README.md).

## Every line, every axis

Review each line for meaning, tone/connotation, its place in the story, scansion, stress and
rhyme. Review the whole song too: section jobs, callbacks, repeated patterns and what the
listener will learn or do differently. Text checks support the review; a generated take and
the expert ear establish the sung phrasing.

Repair the specific problem while preserving successful craft. Read the
[meaning and teaching guidance](docs/lyrics/meaning.md#every-line-every-axis) when drafting
claims or revising after expert feedback.

## Meaning first

[Meaning, voice and teaching](docs/lyrics/meaning.md) covers natural phrases, literal and implied
claims, technical connotations, concrete examples, mishearings and attributed voices.
Read it when deciding what a line says and what a listener will take from it.

## Rhythm

[Rhythm, rhyme and patter](docs/lyrics/rhythm-and-rhyme.md#rhythm) covers rhyme families,
accent, predictable rhyme slots, stress, syllable budgets and deliberate rhythm bends.
Read it when choosing or checking a recurring pattern.

## Patter songs

Read the [patter guidance](docs/lyrics/rhythm-and-rhyme.md#patter-songs) for density, term
placement and precise answering grids. Use the
[parallel-drafting method](.claude/skills/songwriting/references/drafting-with-parallel-drafters.md)
when sections can be drafted independently from a shared grid and meaning ledger.

## Structure

[Structure and examples](docs/lyrics/structure-and-process.md#structure) covers section jobs,
context, payoffs, refrains and genre-specific form. Episode 1’s pop form is an example;
a different song can use a different form.

## Process

[Revision and scratchbooks](docs/lyrics/structure-and-process.md#process) covers preserving
drafts, keeping the lyric ahead of the storyboard and bringing complete options to the expert.
New lessons belong in the reference that owns the decision; see
[maintaining this guide](docs/lyrics/structure-and-process.md#maintaining-the-guide).

## Checking

Use the [checking guidance](docs/lyrics/rhythm-and-rhyme.md#checking) for dictionary limitations,
grid comparisons and review after a fix. The rhyme tool compares vowels and stresses;
matching consonant endings distinguishes perfect rhyme from assonance.

## Writing for the performer

[Performer guidance](docs/lyrics/performers.md) records what each generator demonstrated.
Suno repeated a verse melody under new words in episode 3 when the beat grid matched exactly;
MiniMax needed a different structure in episode 1. Check phrase joins in the actual take.

## Generating the song (MiniMax)

The [dated MiniMax findings](docs/lyrics/performers.md#generating-the-song-minimax) preserve
section tags, pronunciation fixes, punctuation, held-note workarounds and uneven takes.
Use them for that performer and problem; they do not constrain Suno or human singers.
