The earlier ["What Happens If?" treatment](04-what-happens-if.md) holds the expert notes behind this episode; the [scratchbook](04-scratchbook.md) preserves replaced lyrics and alternatives. The earlier teaching notes' references to Clawd describe the same boastful agent role.

An agent boasts about passing “tests”. A testing crew punctures that confidence, follows a disappearing gym record and shows how agents can investigate together. The practical lesson: **give testing agents tools, users, qualities to investigate and ways to judge what happens; let them follow discoveries; request findings, evidence and limits.**

Episodes 2 and 3 become practical equipment: qualities guide what the crew investigates; oracles help it judge what happens. Checking verifies specified claims and remains useful within that broader investigation. The misconception is "It wrote tests and they all pass, so it's tested."

## Musical direction

A Cole Porter inspired swing revue: urbane confidence, conversational instructions, precise internal rhymes, curious investigators and a spacious refrain. Original lyrics; no borrowed lyric or tune. Proposed arrangement: a small jazz orchestra, brushed drums, walking bass, piano, muted brass and clarinet. The builder boasts; the testing crew addresses the person briefing it; the crew then demonstrates the work.

Proposed tempo: 132 bpm in 4/4. Dense patter uses internal rhymes in recurring slots, with contrasting section shapes and a spacious six-phrase refrain. Each line on this sheet is a sung breathing phrase. The song map will establish the setting and duration.

## What changes in the brief

Before:

> Write tests. Make sure they pass.

After:

> Have a separate testing crew investigate my app with a browser and testing playbook. Here are its users, the situations they'll use it in, the qualities that matter, and references to help you judge what happens. Follow clues and change your investigation as you learn. Show me what you tried, what you found, the evidence, and what's still unknown. Ask for context you need from me.

For your own app, supply the actual users, situations, qualities and references. For the gym log, those might be a person recording a workout without signal, reliable saving, and their written workout notes. For the private chat, privacy matters, and a list of permitted readers helps the investigator judge what other accounts can see. Those are starting points; agents develop further questions from what they discover.

## Teaching notes

The opening and first verse use “test” in Clawd's own everyday vocabulary, before the crew explains the checking/testing distinction. The first three uses carry quotation marks to signal the claim being questioned; they add no sung words. “The marks decide how tests get made” is unquoted: Clawd believes it is writing actual tests, without considering the chorus's “actually test it” challenge. The opening recognises a green test report. In the first verse the agent copies the app's calculation into a check, so their agreement can share a mistake. A screenshot difference then earns a change to what the check expects; the agent changes the reference rather than investigating the changed app output. Finally, the agent explains that it was trained to make the grade, so passing-score incentives shape the checks it writes. The refrain supplies the investigative move: find a clue and pursue its implications.

The gym verse demonstrates that move. A record disappears after reloading; reconnecting does not recover it. The crew tries a new save with connectivity, then repeats the offline condition and makes a check for the discovered problem. The song shows checking as useful within an investigation, rather than making checks the whole mission.

The bridge explicitly addresses briefing a fresh testing crew, then supplies the practical equipment: browsers, a playbook, users, goals and oracles. The chat and cart questions demonstrate different investigations; the crew shares observations and asks for human interpretation where needed. The outro reports the gym loss and explicitly leaves access to the log uninvestigated. The after-prompt collects these instructions into a brief a listener can adapt.

The breakdown contrasts applying a rule already set with developing further questions and experiments as clues appear. Its callback to the copied sums distinguishes reporting agreement from investigating whether that agreement hides a shared error. Checks contribute to testing; evaluating the app also requires interpreting what matters for its users.
